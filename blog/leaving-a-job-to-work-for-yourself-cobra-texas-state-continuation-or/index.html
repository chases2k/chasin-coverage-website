Leaving a Job to Work for Yourself? COBRA, Texas State Continuation, or Your Own Health Insurance Plan

Chase TaborLicensed health insurance agent
November 23, 20266 min read

The short answer

When you leave a job to work for yourself, you do not have to keep your old employer's coverage through COBRA. You can continue that group insurance plan under federal COBRA or Texas state continuation, join a spouse's work insurance plan, or buy your own individual health insurance plan. The right fit depends on your former employer's size, your yearly deductible progress, your health history, and what the full monthly bill looks like once your employer stops helping pay it.

Federal COBRA keeps your old coverage at full cost

COBRA (Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act) is the federal law that lets former workers temporarily keep their old job's group health insurance plan. Under the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) Health care coverage guide, federal COBRA applies to employers with 20 or more employees and covers people leaving a job for reasons other than gross misconduct. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) COBRA Q&A confirms this applies whether leaving is voluntary or involuntary, and coverage lasts 18 months for the former employee.

The shock for new owners is the monthly premium (the monthly price of an insurance plan). HealthCare.gov's COBRA glossary explains that someone electing COBRA pays 100% of the premiums, including the employer's share, plus a small administrative fee. CMS notes the total COBRA rate cannot exceed 100 percent of the group cost plus 2 percent for administrative costs.

The clock gives you room to compare first. TDI states an employer must send continuation notice within 30 days after a job ends. CMS explains former workers get at least 60 days to elect COBRA, and the group insurance plan cannot require the first payment earlier than 45 days after election. Once elected and paid for, COBRA is retroactive to the date job coverage ended.

COBRA also has limits. TDI notes COBRA ends if your former employer stops offering health insurance altogether, and if you keep an HMO (health maintenance organization) and move outside its service area, it pays only for emergency care.

Texas state continuation covers smaller companies and extra months

Many Texans leave companies with fewer than 20 employees, where federal COBRA does not apply. TDI's guide explains that workers ineligible for federal COBRA can use Texas state continuation to keep their group insurance plan for nine months after leaving, if they had coverage on it for the three months right before their job ended.

State continuation can also extend federal COBRA. TDI states qualifying Texas group insurance plans must allow six extra months after 18 months of federal COBRA, totaling 24 months for the former employee.

Limits apply here too. TDI explains the group insurance plan must follow Texas insurance laws, so state continuation does not cover self-funded employer coverage (where the employer pays claims directly). Someone usually cannot get state continuation if fired, and the former employee pays the full cost.

Dropping COBRA early affects Marketplace timing

Losing job-based health insurance opens a 60-day Special Enrollment Period for a Marketplace insurance plan. HealthCare.gov's COBRA coverage page states this 60-day window applies when job coverage ends or COBRA runs its full course, not when someone cancels COBRA early.

HealthCare.gov states directly: "Voluntarily dropping COBRA doesn't count. Choosing to stop paying COBRA premiums on your own doesn't qualify." Ending COBRA mid-year usually means waiting until the next Open Enrollment period for a Marketplace insurance plan unless another life event occurs. HealthCare.gov's job-loss page adds that Marketplace insurance plans start the first day of the month after job coverage ends. Chase works with private insurance plans outside the Marketplace and refers Marketplace cases out, so comparing options before electing COBRA keeps every path open.

Individual private insurance plans fit some buyers and not others

Individual health insurance is coverage you buy on your own instead of through an employer. Buying outside the Marketplace can fit self-employed owners who do not receive Marketplace tax credits, since TDI notes tax credits require buying through the federal Marketplace.

Rules differ by coverage type. Under HealthCare.gov's pre-existing conditions rules, Marketplace and standard major medical individual insurance plans cannot reject applicants or charge more for a preexisting condition (a health issue you had before coverage starts).

Non-traditional or medically underwritten private options work differently. TDI's guide to alternative health coverage warns that alternative options may exclude preexisting and chronic conditions, carry waiting periods or payment limits, and do not allow federal subsidies. An underwritten private insurance plan may fit a healthy self-employed buyer weighing options beyond COBRA, while someone with ongoing medical needs generally needs major medical coverage that cannot decline preexisting conditions.

Four checks before picking your next insurance plan

  1. Check your deductible progress. A deductible is what you pay before your insurance plan starts paying. COBRA keeps this year's progress, while a new individual insurance plan resets it. See deductible vs. out-of-pocket maximum.
  2. Check a spouse's employer coverage. Losing work coverage may open a window to join their group insurance plan. See choosing between a spouse's work insurance plan and your own.
  3. Get your end date and COBRA rate in writing from HR.
  4. Check doctors, medicines, and licensing. Confirm your doctors are in network (contracted with that insurance plan) and your medicines are on its drug list. TDI reminds Texans to buy only from licensed companies and licensed agents.

FAQ

How long do I have to elect COBRA after leaving my job?

Under CMS and TDI rules, you get at least 60 days to elect COBRA and at least 45 days after electing to make the first payment.

What if my Texas employer had fewer than 20 employees?

Federal COBRA generally starts at 20 workers. Under TDI rules, Texas state continuation may cover a Texas-regulated group insurance plan for nine months if you had coverage for the three prior months, though self-funded coverage is exempt.

Should I cancel COBRA before my new insurance plan starts?

No. HealthCare.gov advises confirming your new start date before ending COBRA to avoid a coverage gap.
